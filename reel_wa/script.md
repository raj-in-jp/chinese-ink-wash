Nihon. "The origin of the sun." Because seen from China, the sun rises over Japan. Chinese records even say the Japanese disliked the old name, because it was not
elegant.

## Fact notes

- 607: the embassy of Ono no Imoko to Sui China.  The Book of Sui (隋書, 倭國傳) quotes the letter as
  「日出處天子致書日沒處天子無恙」 and records that Emperor Yang was displeased (帝覽之不悅).
- 倭: the name used in Chinese histories (Book of Han, Wei zhi, Book of Sui).  The character's sense is usually given as
  "compliant / submissive"; reading it as "dwarf" (矮) is a later association, hence "many read it as".
- Around 700: the Old Book of Tang (舊唐書) and New Book of Tang record the change to 日本; the Japanese envoy of 702
  (Awata no Mahito) is the first known to use it with China.  舊唐書: 「倭國自惡其名不雅，改為日本」.
- 和 replaced 倭 for the native name Yamato/Wa in the 8th century (e.g. 大和), keeping the reading "wa".
- 和食 (Japanese food), 和紙 (Japanese paper), 令和 (era name from 1 May 2019).
- "Land of the Rising Sun": 日本 literally "sun's origin".  The name only makes sense looking east from the continent; the
  New Book of Tang (新唐書) records that the Japanese said they took it because the country is near where the sun rises
  (「國近日所出，以爲名」).  "Land of the Rising Sun" itself is the English rendering.
- Reiwa (令和) was taken from the Man'yōshū, from the preface to its 32 poems on plum blossoms.
