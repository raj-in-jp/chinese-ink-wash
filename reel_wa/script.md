## Narration (recorded; Scribe word-exact, English and Japanese)

In 607, Japan sent China a letter so bold, the Chinese emperor was furious. It began: "From the Son of Heaven where
the sun rises... to the Son of Heaven where the sun sets."
      [607 slams in; the scroll 日出處天子致書日沒處天子無恙 unrolls; FURIOUS!; a sun rises over Japan, sets over China]
Why so bold? For centuries, China had called Japan Wa. Written with a character that meant something like
"submissive". And later, many read it as "dwarf".
      [WHY SO BOLD?; 倭 brushed, WA; "SUBMISSIVE" stamped; 倭 shrinks; "DWARF"?!]
Japan wanted a better name. So, around the year 700, Japanese envoys told China: we are not Wa anymore. We are
Nihon. "The origin of the sun." Chinese records even say the Japanese disliked the old name, because it was not
elegant.
      [倭 crossed out; AROUND 700; the envoy ship: NOT 倭 ANYMORE!; 日本 over a red sun, 日 = SUN · 本 = ORIGIN;
       the record 倭國自惡其名不雅; NOT ELEGANT!]
But they kept the old sound, Wa. They just swapped the kanji, for one that means "harmony".
      [SAME SOUND わ WA; the card flips 倭 → 和; 和 = HARMONY]
And that kanji is still everywhere today. Washoku, Japanese food. Washi, Japanese paper. Even Reiwa, the name of
this era.
      [和 IS EVERYWHERE; cards 和食 WASHOKU, 和紙 WASHI, 令和 REIWA 2019]

## Fact notes

- 607: the embassy of Ono no Imoko to Sui China.  The Book of Sui (隋書, 倭國傳) quotes the letter as
  「日出處天子致書日沒處天子無恙」 and records that Emperor Yang was displeased (帝覽之不悅).
- 倭: the name used in Chinese histories (Book of Han, Wei zhi, Book of Sui).  The character's sense is usually given as
  "compliant / submissive"; reading it as "dwarf" (矮) is a later association, hence "many read it as".
- Around 700: the Old Book of Tang (舊唐書) and New Book of Tang record the change to 日本; the Japanese envoy of 702
  (Awata no Mahito) is the first known to use it with China.  舊唐書: 「倭國自惡其名不雅，改為日本」.
- 和 replaced 倭 for the native name Yamato/Wa in the 8th century (e.g. 大和), keeping the reading "wa".
- 和食 (Japanese food), 和紙 (Japanese paper), 令和 (era name from 1 May 2019).
