## Narration
Read this. Big… harmony? だいわ (dai-wa)? Nope. It's read… やまと (Yamato). And not one sound matches the kanji!
That's because やまと (Yamato) is older than writing in Japan. It was the name of one small region. Today, that's
なら (Nara). The first rulers of Japan lived there, so the name grew to mean the whole country.
Then kanji arrived from China. Japan kept the old Japanese word, and just picked kanji for the meaning. First, this one
(倭). Then, in the seven hundreds, these two (大和). "Great harmony."
So you read the meaning, not the sound. Japanese does this a lot. Today is… きょう (kyō, 今日). Adult is… おとな (otona, 大人).
And what does やまと (Yamato) itself mean? Nobody knows for sure. Maybe "mountain gate". Maybe "the place of the mountains".
You still hear it today. The Japanese spirit… やまとだましい (Yamato-damashii, 大和魂). And… やまとなでしこ (Yamato
nadeshiko, 大和撫子), named after a delicate pink flower. Follow for more. またね！

## Fact notes
- Yamato: originally a region (present-day Nara Prefecture), seat of the early Yamato kingship; the name came to stand
  for Japan as a whole.
- Written 倭, then 大倭; in the 8th century the province name was officially written 大和 (read Yamato throughout).
- 大和 is read as a whole word (jukujikun-style): kanji for meaning, native reading — like 今日 kyō and 大人 otona.
- The etymology of "Yamato" is uncertain; 山門 "mountain gate" and 山処 "mountain place" are common theories (hence "maybe").
- 大和魂: "Japanese spirit".  大和撫子: an old ideal of a graceful Japanese woman, named after the nadeshiko (Dianthus
  superbus), a delicate pink fringed flower.
